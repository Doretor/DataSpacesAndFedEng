TASK 3: TEMPORAL FILTERING ANALYSIS

Scenario: We are checking satellite photos for a hypothetical incident that happened on January 5, 2024.

1. Pre-event window (Before the incident: Jan 1 to Jan 4):
- Number of photos found: 2
- Exact dates: January 2 and January 4.
- Status: We have good pictures showing how the area looked just before the event happened.

2. Event window (The exact day of the incident: Jan 5):
- Number of photos found: 0
- Exact dates: None.
- Status: The satellite did not fly over this area on January 5, so we don't have a picture of the exact moment it started.

3. Post-event window (After the incident: Jan 6 to Jan 10):
- Number of photos found: 2
- Exact dates: January 7 and January 9.
- Status: We have pictures showing the damage or changes a few days after the event.

4. Time gaps identified:
There is a 3-day gap between January 4 and January 7 with no photos at all. 

5. Can we use this satellite to monitor disasters?
Yes, but mostly to compare the "before and after" situations (for example, to see how much of a forest burned down). 
It is not good for watching a fast-moving crisis live, because there are days when the satellite is simply not looking at our area.

6. Is getting a picture every few days enough?
Getting a new photo every 2 or 3 days is great for watching slow things like farming, seasons changing, or big floods. 
However, it is too slow if we need to quickly track a moving wildfire or a sudden explosion.

7. How can we fix this problem?
If we need updates every day or during bad weather, we cannot rely only on standard cameras. 
We should also use radar satellites (like Sentinel-1). Radars can take pictures more often, at night, and they can see straight through the clouds.
